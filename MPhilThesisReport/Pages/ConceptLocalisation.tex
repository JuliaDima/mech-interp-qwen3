\chapter{Concept Emergence via Contrastive Residual-Stream Analysis}
\label{sec:concept_localisation}

This chapter studies where arithmetic concepts appear in the residual stream of
Qwen3-4B. We analyse three controlled problems. The first is carry detection in
addition. The second is divisibility by seven through a greatest common divisor
prompt. The third is membership in one residue class modulo seven. Each task asks
the model to compute a relation over numbers, not only to match a surface form.

Accuracy alone does not identify the internal computation. A model can answer a
prompt correctly while using a template cue, a memorised association, or a partial
calculation that does not match the intended concept. A linear probe gives a
useful first signal, but it can also detect information that was computed earlier
and merely carried forward. We therefore combine contrastive residual-stream
directions, anchor selection, null permutations, causal patching, and transcoder
feature projections. The aim is to locate the layer and position where a concept
is represented, and then test whether that representation changes the answer.

The analysis follows prior work on activation-space directions and causal
representation editing in language models
\citep{zou2025representationengineeringtopdownapproach,
panickssery2024steeringllama2contrastive,
meng2023locatingeditingfactualassociations,fear2025physics}.

\section{Contrastive Dataset}
\label{sec:concept_pairs}

For each concept we build matched prompt pairs \((x_i^+,x_i^-)\). The positive
prompt contains the target property. The negative prompt removes that property
while preserving the syntax, length, scale, and answer format as closely as
possible. This prevents the mean residual-stream difference from being dominated
by incidental token changes.

The prompt template is retained only when Qwen3-4B reaches at least 10\%
first-token accuracy. This threshold verifies that the model engages with the
format before we interpret internal activations. Since the first answer token
expresses the result, interventions are evaluated by their effect on that token.

\section{Residual-Stream Concept Delta}
\label{sec:concept_delta}

Let \(h_{l,t}(x) \in \mathbb{R}^d\) be the residual-stream vector at layer \(l\)
and token position \(t\). We do not fix one anchor position in advance. Instead,
we scan candidate positions \(\mathcal{T}\), since an operand, delimiter, or final
token can carry different parts of the computation.

For anchor \(t^*\), the layer-wise concept delta is
\begin{equation}
\delta_l
=
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} h_{l,t^*}(x_i^+)
-
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} h_{l,t^*}(x_i^-)
=
\mathbb{E}[h_{l,t^*}(x^+)]-\mathbb{E}[h_{l,t^*}(x^-)].
\label{eq:concept_delta}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, if \(\Delta h_{i,l}=h_{l,t^*}(x_i^+)-h_{l,t^*}(x_i^-)\), then
\(\delta_l=N^{-1}\sum_i \Delta h_{i,l}\).

The trajectory \((\delta_l)_{l=0}^{L-1}\) tracks how the contrast changes through
the network. Under the linear representation hypothesis \citep{park2023linear},
\(\delta_l\) approximates a concept axis when the contrast is controlled and the
concept is linearly separable at that layer.

\section{Layer and Anchor Metrics}
\label{sec:delta_metrics}

The first statistic is the delta norm
\begin{equation}
D_l = \lVert \delta_l \rVert_2.
\label{eq:delta_norm}
\end{equation}
Raw residual norms tend to grow with depth. We therefore divide by the mean
activation norm at the same layer,
\begin{equation}
\mu_l^{\mathrm{act}} = \mathbb{E}_{x}\!\left[\lVert h_{l,t^*}(x)\rVert_2\right],
\label{eq:mean_act_norm}
\end{equation}
and use
\begin{equation}
D_l^{\mathrm{act}} = \frac{D_l}{\mu_l^{\mathrm{act}} + \varepsilon}.
\label{eq:act_norm_delta}
\end{equation}
To compare anchors with different absolute scale, each trajectory is divided by
its own maximum,
\begin{equation}
\tilde{D}_l^{\mathrm{act}}
=
\frac{D_l^{\mathrm{act}}}{\max_j D_j^{\mathrm{act}} + \varepsilon}
\in [0,1].
\label{eq:double_norm}
\end{equation}

A peak in \(\tilde{D}_l^{\mathrm{act}}\) marks a layer where the concept delta is
large relative to the local residual-stream scale. Figure~\ref{fig:norm_comparison}
shows why this normalisation is needed.

\begin{figure}[ht!]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Pages/Figures/norm_comparison.pdf}
  \caption{Raw and activation-normalised delta norms for two anchors.  The raw
  curve grows with depth in both cases, while the normalised curve separates a
  broad carry direction from a localised GCD transition.}
  \label{fig:norm_comparison}
\end{figure}

We also measure how concentrated the delta norm is across layers. Let
\(l^*=\arg\max_l D_l\). The sharpness score is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{sharp}(t^*)
=
\frac{\sum_{l=\max(0,l^*-1)}^{\min(L-1,l^*+1)} D_l}
     {\sum_{l=0}^{L-1} D_l + \varepsilon}.
\label{eq:sharpness}
\end{equation}
High sharpness indicates a narrow processing window. Low sharpness can indicate a
diffuse direction, weak separation, or a stable representation present across many
layers.

The inter-layer cosine matrix measures whether the direction itself is stable,
\begin{equation}
C_{l,m}
=
\frac{\delta_l \cdot \delta_m}
       {\lVert\delta_l\rVert_2\,\lVert\delta_m\rVert_2 + \varepsilon}.
\label{eq:layer_cosine}
\end{equation}
A high-similarity block indicates one stable axis. A two-block structure indicates
a rotation at the boundary between blocks. A diffuse diagonal indicates gradual
rotation with no single transition layer.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Pages/Figures/cosim_patterns_abc.pdf}
  \caption{Normalised delta trajectories and inter-layer cosine matrices.  Carry
  shows a stable direction, GCD shows a block transition, and residue class stays

  \label{fig:cosim_patterns}

\citet{panickssery2024steeringllama2contrastive} report a diffuse diagonal pattern
for steering vectors in Llama 2. The residue-class heatmap has the same form. The
carry and GCD heatmaps are more structured, which shows that arithmetic anchors
can produce stable or sharply rotating directions rather than only slow drift.

To select anchors, we compute the non-monotonicity score
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{nm}(t^*)
=
\max_{l}
\min\!\left(
  c_l - \min_{j < l} c_j,\quad
  c_l - \min_{j > l} c_j
\right),
\label{eq:nonmonotonicity}
\end{equation}
where \(c_l = \tilde{D}_l^{\mathrm{act}}\). This is the prominence of the largest
local peak. Monotone curves score near zero. Isolated peaks score high.

\subsection{Permutation Null}
\label{sec:null_permutation}

A contrastive scan can create apparent directions even when labels are unrelated
to the concept. We estimate this background by permuting positive and negative
labels and recomputing the trajectory. For permutation \(\pi\), let
\(\delta_l^\pi\) be the resulting delta. The null distribution for statistic
\(q_l\) is
\begin{equation}
\left\{q_l(\delta^{\pi}) : \pi=1,\ldots,P\right\}.
\label{eq:null_distribution}
\end{equation}

Observed scores are compared with the null mean and quantiles. A layer outside
the null range is less likely to reflect generic prompt variation. A layer inside
the null range is not evidence of absence. It only means the tested contrast did
not produce a discriminative direction at that anchor.

\section{Causal Tests of the Residual Direction}
\label{sec:causal_validation}

\subsection{Positive-to-Negative Activation Patching}
\label{sec:act_patching}

Activation patching tests causal sufficiency. For each pair \((x_i^+,x_i^-)\) and
layer \(l\), the positive residual vector at the anchor is inserted into the
negative forward pass,
\[
h_{l,t^*}(x_i^-) \leftarrow h_{l,t^*}(x_i^+).
\]
Let \(m(x)=\ell_{\mathrm{pos}}(x)-\ell_{\mathrm{neg}}(x)\) be the answer logit
margin at the final prediction position. The patching score is
\begin{equation}
S_l^{\mathrm{patch}}
=
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\left[m\!\left(\tilde{x}_{i,[l]}^-\right)-m(x_i^-)\right],
\label{eq:patch_score}
\end{equation}
where \(\tilde{x}_{i,[l]}^-\) is the negative prompt with the layer-\(l\) anchor
activation patched from the positive prompt. A positive score means that the
patched state moves the output toward the positive answer.

\subsection{First-Order Causal Approximation}
\label{sec:grad_dot_delta}

Gradient-dot-delta approximates patching. For each negative prompt, we compute the
gradient of the margin with respect to the anchor residual stream and dot it with
the mean concept delta,
\begin{equation}
S_l^{\mathrm{grad}}
=
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\nabla_{h_{l,t^*}}m(x_i^- )\cdot\delta_l.
\label{eq:grad_delta_score}
\end{equation}
The first-order relation is
\begin{equation}
m(\tilde{x}_{i,[l]}^-)
\approx
m(x_i^-)
+
\nabla_{h_{l,t^*}}m(x_i^-)
\cdot
\left(h_{l,t^*}(x_i^+) - h_{l,t^*}(x_i^-)\right).
\label{eq:patch_taylor}
\end{equation}
Agreement with patching indicates local linearity along the tested direction.
Disagreement indicates higher-order effects or a poor mean-delta summary.

\section{Peak-Layer Scoring}
\label{sec:peak_selection}

Candidate peak layers combine geometry, the null baseline, and causal effect. Let
\(e_l=\max(0,D_l^{\mathrm{act}}-\mathrm{null}_l)\). The combined score is
\begin{equation}
S_l
=
\tilde{D}_l^{\mathrm{act}}
+
e_l
+
S_l^{\mathrm{patch}}
+
0.25\, S_l^{\mathrm{grad}},
\label{eq:combined_peak_score}
\end{equation}
with each term normalised by its own maximum. The gradient term is down-weighted
because it is only a first-order approximation. For anchors with mean pairwise
cosine above \(0.5\), the trajectory term is omitted, since a stable direction does
not by itself isolate a processing layer. Peaks are then selected with prominence
threshold \(0.08\) and minimum distance \(\max(3,\lfloor L/8\rfloor)\) layers.

\section{Transcoder Feature Projection}
\label{sec:feature_projection}

Residual-stream deltas give dense directions. To connect these directions to
transcoder features, we compare \(\delta_l\) with each feature's decoder and encoder
vectors. The decoder vector measures whether the feature writes along the concept
direction; the encoder vector provides a secondary sensitivity proxy.

\paragraph{Decoder output alignment.}
The transcoder decomposes the post-block residual as
\begin{equation}
h_l^{\mathrm{post}}(x)
= h_l^{\mathrm{mid}}(x) + \sum_f z_{l,f}(x)\,w_{l,f}^{\mathrm{dec}},
\label{eq:transcoder_decomp}
\end{equation}
where \(h_l^{\mathrm{mid}}\) is the residual entering the MLP block.
Taking the contrastive mean difference,
\begin{equation}
\delta_l^{\mathrm{post}}
= \delta_l^{\mathrm{mid}} + \sum_f \Delta z_{l,f}\,w_{l,f}^{\mathrm{dec}},
\label{eq:delta_decomp}
\end{equation}
with \(\Delta z_{l,f}=\mathbb{E}[z_{l,f}(x^+)]-\mathbb{E}[z_{l,f}(x^-)]\).
Projecting onto a decoder vector \(w_{l,f'}^{\mathrm{dec}}\) gives
\begin{equation}
\delta_l^{\mathrm{post}}\cdot w_{l,f'}^{\mathrm{dec}}
=
\delta_l^{\mathrm{mid}}\cdot w_{l,f'}^{\mathrm{dec}}
+
\sum_f \Delta z_{l,f}
\underbrace{\bigl(w_{l,f}^{\mathrm{dec}}\cdot w_{l,f'}^{\mathrm{dec}}\bigr)}_{\text{decoder Gram matrix}},
\label{eq:dec_gram}
\end{equation}
or in matrix form \(\delta_l^{\mathrm{post}} W^{\mathrm{dec}\top} = \delta_l^{\mathrm{mid}} W^{\mathrm{dec}\top} + \Delta z_l\,(W^{\mathrm{dec}}W^{\mathrm{dec}\top})\).
The projection score therefore conflates a feature's direct contribution with
cross-feature interference weighted by the Gram matrix. We use the normalised cosine
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{cos}_{l,f}^{\mathrm{dec}}
=
\frac{w_{l,f}^{\mathrm{dec}} \cdot \delta_l}
     {\lVert w_{l,f}^{\mathrm{dec}}\rVert_2\,\lVert\delta_l\rVert_2 + \varepsilon}
\label{eq:decoder_cosine}
\end{equation}
as a practical ranking score, bearing in mind this Gram-matrix entanglement.

\paragraph{Encoder input alignment.}
Because \(\delta_l\) is measured on the output residual \(h_l^{\mathrm{post}}\)
rather than the input \(h_l^{\mathrm{mid}}\), the encoder alignment is a secondary
proxy. The mean contrastive activation difference \(\Delta z_{l,f}\) projected onto the
encoder direction gives \(\Delta z_{l,f}\cdot w_{l,f}^{\mathrm{enc}}\cdot\delta_l\),
which indicates how sensitively feature \(f\) would respond if the concept direction
were present in the MLP input. We report the normalised form
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{cos}_{l,f}^{\mathrm{enc}}
=
\frac{w_{l,f}^{\mathrm{enc}} \cdot \delta_l}
     {\lVert w_{l,f}^{\mathrm{enc}}\rVert_2\,\lVert\delta_l\rVert_2 + \varepsilon}
\label{eq:encoder_cosine}
\end{equation}
as a heuristic complement to the decoder score.

\paragraph{Combined feature score.}
We rank features by the sum of decoder and encoder alignment,
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{l,f}
=
\operatorname{cos}_{l,f}^{\mathrm{dec}}
+
\operatorname{cos}_{l,f}^{\mathrm{enc}}.
\label{eq:combined_cos}
\end{equation}
Positive scores align with concept presence. Negative scores align with concept
absence. The positive and negative pools are ranked by \(|\sigma_{l,f}|\), merged,
deduplicated, and filtered to active features.

\section{Feature-Direction Interventions}
\label{sec:interventions}

We test projected features by modifying the raw MLP output along their decoder
directions. We avoid full transcoder reconstruction because it degraded
first-token accuracy in preliminary runs. The intervention therefore changes only
the nominated feature direction and leaves the rest of the MLP output intact.

\subsection{Feature-Output Ablation}
\label{sec:ablation}

For feature \(f\) with decoder direction \(w_{l,f}^{\mathrm{dec}}\), a pre-hook
computes its activation \(z_{l,f}(x)\). A post-hook subtracts the selected part of
its decoder contribution,
\begin{equation}
y_l(x) \leftarrow y_l(x) - (1-\alpha)\, z_{l,f}(x)\, w_{l,f}^{\mathrm{dec}},
\label{eq:ablation}
\end{equation}
where \(\alpha=0\) gives full ablation and \(\alpha=1\) leaves the model unchanged.

\subsection{Decoder-Direction Injection}
\label{sec:injection}

Injection adds the decoder direction at a fixed token position whether or not the
feature fires naturally,
\begin{equation}
y_l(x) \leftarrow y_l(x) + \delta_f\, w_{l,f}^{\mathrm{dec}}.
\label{eq:injection}
\end{equation}
The magnitude \(\delta_f\) is the mean natural activation
\(\bar{a}_f^+=\mathbb{E}[z_{l,f}(x^+)]\) from the concept scan. Ablation tests
whether the model loses the behaviour when the feature output is removed. Injection
tests whether the behaviour appears when the feature output is added.

\section{Empirical Results}
\label{sec:concept_results}

\subsection{Carry Detection in Three-Digit Addition}
\label{sec:carry_results}

The carry task tests whether the units digits satisfy
\((A \bmod 10)+(B \bmod 10)\geq 10\). Qwen3-4B reaches 100\%
teacher-forced accuracy on the selected carry-positive and carry-negative prompts.

The strongest concept direction appears at the units digit of the second operand,
raw position 9 and model position 10 after the sink token. The main peaks are at
layers 4 and 5, with secondary peaks at layers 19 and 22. The cosine heatmap shows
a two-block transition near layers 4 and 5, followed by a stable carry direction in
later layers.

Feature projection identifies four aligned features,
\(\texttt{L4\_F126502}\), \(\texttt{L5\_F121933}\),
\(\texttt{L19\_F23877}\), and \(\texttt{L22\_F67294}\), with decoder-plus-encoder
scores \(0.84\), \(0.63\), \(0.72\), and \(0.62\). They fire on 23 of 99 positive
pairs at the units-digit anchor and on fewer than five negative pairs.

\subsubsection*{Joint carry-feature ablation}

Joint ablation of the four features with \(\alpha=0\) reduces positive-example
accuracy from 100\% to 47.8\% on the 23 prompts where at least one feature fires.
Negative-example accuracy is unchanged. Individual ablations have no measurable
effect, which indicates functional redundancy.

The altered predictions in Table~\ref{tab:carry_intervention} shift by about
\(-8\) to \(-11\). These offsets are close to the units digit of the second
operand. This suggests that the features carry part of the digit-level value used
by the running sum. In two prompts with hundreds-digit carries, \(\texttt{948+904}\)
and \(\texttt{902+978}\), ablation shifts the output by \(-200\), suggesting a
connection to higher-order carry propagation.

\subsubsection*{Decoder-direction carry injection}

Injecting the four decoder directions at the ones$_b$ anchor with fixed magnitude
\(\delta=5.0\) into 198 carry-negative prompts reduces accuracy from 100\% to
22.2\%. It changes 154 predictions. The model behaves as if a carry were present
even though \(a_0+b_0<10\). In Table~\ref{tab:carry_intervention}, the output
usually increases by \(+7\) or \(+8\), and the units digit moves toward 9.

Ablation removes a real carry signal. Injection creates a false one. The two
effects show that these decoder directions are causally tied to a carry-increment
operation. Since the features fire on only 23 of 99 positive pairs, they form one
carry pathway rather than the full carry mechanism.

\subsection{Fourier Structure in Carry Feature Grids}
\label{sec:fourier_patterns}

Among the transcoder features that activate at the carry anchor, a notable subset exhibits
structured responses over the \((a, b)\) operand grid that extend beyond a simple
carry/no-carry boundary. Rather than activating in a half-plane partition
\(\{a_0 + b_0 \geq 10\}\), these features tile the grid with smooth periodic patterns
consistent with a two-dimensional Fourier basis. Notably, many of these patterns do not
directly encode whether a carry occurs: some features respond to the difference
\(a - b\), functioning as operand-comparison detectors; others activate on parity,
firing when \(a + b\) is odd or even. This suggests that the model's feature space at this
position is richer than the carry concept alone, and that arithmetic representations in
Qwen3-4B may emerge as superpositions of more elementary modular coordinates rather than
dedicated single-concept circuits.

For each feature we compute the mean activation \(f(a,b)\) over the \(10\times10\)
ones-digit grid. The natural Fourier basis on this grid,
\[
\exp\!\left(\tfrac{2\pi i}{10}(ua+vb)\right), \quad u,v\in\{0,\ldots,9\},
\]
separates iso-sum modes (\(u=v\)), iso-difference modes (\(u=-v\)), single-operand
modes, and a parity mode at \((u,v)=(5,5)\). We score each feature by how much of its
activation power concentrates in these structured modes, retaining those above a
threshold of \(S_f > 0.6\).

Fourier-structured features appear only at the ones$_b$ anchor, clustering in layers
12--14 and layer 21, downstream of the layer 4--5 peak in the concept-direction
analysis. Figure~\ref{fig:fourier_selection} shows eight selected features; dominant
patterns are iso-sum or iso-difference with \(R^2\geq0.85\) using one to four modes.

\paragraph{Fourier-feature ablation.}
Joint ablation of all 60 Fourier-structured features leaves accuracy at 100\% with a
mean probability change of \(+0.003\). The Fourier features are geometrically present
but causally dispensable: the carry signal is either distributed across a larger feature
set or supported by redundant mechanisms outside the selected subset.
