\abstract{%
  Whether a language model that produces correct answers to arithmetic problems genuinely
  internalises the underlying computation, or relies on surface patterns from training,
  cannot be determined from outputs alone. This thesis examines that question in Qwen3-4B,
  an independently trained instruction-tuned model, through two complementary directions.

  The first reproduces the circuit-tracing methodology from Anthropic's
  \textit{On the Biology of a Large Language Model}, applying it to a model outside
  the Claude family. We find that analogous arithmetic circuit structure is present,
  with the residual stream encoding operand values at identifiable token positions and
  attribution graphs recovering the same high-level topology. A multilingual experiment
  confirms that the operation, operand, and output-language components of the circuit
  can be independently suppressed and reinstated, with interventions transferring across
  English, Chinese, and French. These results indicate that the mechanistic organisation
  identified by Lindsey et al.\ is not specific to a single model family.

  The second introduces a method for localising abstract concepts within the residual stream
  via contrastive prompt pairs, designed to generalise to any domain in which concepts can be
  expressed through matched inputs. Applied to three arithmetic tasks of increasing representational complexity
  (carry detection, GCD divisibility, and residue class membership),
  it identifies the geometric signature of each concept and characterises its localisability across tokens and layers.
  The analysis surfaces structured transcoder features encoding mathematical relationships
  (comparison operators, Fourier decomposition, and divisibility detectors),
  illustrating in a more interpretable form how an instruction-tuned model 
  internalises arithmetic computation.


  In this thesis, we also study the optimisation of learned prefix embedding as a method for knowledge editing, 
  examining whether gradient-based modifications to the input embedding space can adapt 
  specific internal representations without affecting broader model behaviour. 
  We find that while this method generally improves model predictions, 
  the learned embeddings are not interpretable. Instead of encoding identifiable concepts, 
  they appear to encode, in a continuous form, an approximation of the quantity of 
  information the model requires to produce the target output, without correspondence 
  to structured semantic content.
}
